## Priority fixes by reviewer

### openrouter/google/gemini-pro-latest · mobile-dark · 842

A strong, premium mobile interface that masterfully uses color (green/violet) and scale to convey complex financial states. It succeeds in building trust for power users but risks overwhelming beginners with dense information and technical concepts.

- Truncate long hex addresses by default across all screens
- Simplify the onboarding/explanation text for the green/violet rule
- Make the order book easier to read on mobile by increasing row spacing
- Add contextual help for terms like 'Collateral' and 'Credit line'
- Tighten the main action buttons on Home to reveal more context below the fold

### openrouter/google/gemini-pro-latest · desktop-dark · 851

A highly technical, trustworthy, and visually sophisticated interface that perfectly serves power users and liquidity operators. However, its uncompromising commitment to exposing raw mechanics (channels, collateral, frames) creates a steep learning curve that will overwhelm everyday consumers.

- Implement a 'Simple/Advanced' toggle globally to hide route, frame, and collateral mechanics for novice users.
- Add explicit visual directional flow (arrows) to the 'Move' and 'Swap' matrix screens to reduce cognitive load.
- Condense stacked action buttons (like in Assets) into clear primary/secondary hierarchies to prevent choice paralysis.
- Ensure mobile/responsive layouts (currently completely absent in desktop-centric designs) adapt complex tables into stacked cards.
- Replace dense explanatory text blocks with progressive disclosure (tooltips/info icons) to clean up form views.

### openrouter/anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 · desktop-dark · 796

A coherent, premium dark fintech UI with a genuinely clever green/violet scale metaphor consistently applied across screens. It loses points on settings sprawl, destructive-action safety, and a few clipped/cluttered secondary screens (swap-cross, desk, lending) that need tightening before it reads as fully shipped.

- Add a hard confirmation step (typed amount/hold) before any dispute or vault-deletion action
- Fix table/panel clipping on Desk and cross-network swap screens
- Restructure Settings into collapsible sections, isolate destructive vault actions
- Separate testnet faucet UI visually from production balances on Assets screen
- Add inline glossary/tooltips for jargon (lazy entity, quorum, collateral tariff, HTLC legs)

### openrouter/moonshotai/kimi-k3 · desktop-dark · 791

A genuinely distinctive wallet: the one-scale bars, green/violet trust semantics, Before/After account preview and Sovereignty screen are best-in-class for making credit risk visible. It is held back by residual protocol jargon (frames, lanes, entity ids, HTLC 'secret'), inconsistent amount display in lists, clipped Desk numerals, and missing flows (invoice creation, dispute progress, board editing) that several target users need end-to-end.

- De-jargon the surface: replace frames/lanes/entity-id/'secret releases both legs' with plain language plus glossary links; keep hashes behind 'advanced' everywhere (Pay, Receive, Ownership, Desk)
- Fix numeric legibility: scale order-book depth bars to size, never clip Desk table numerals, show amounts on every activity row (filled/closed swaps), render 'You receive' at full strength
- Close the loop on risk CTAs: 'Cover it' as a real button on Home/Account/Sovereignty, feed uncovered debt into NEEDS ATTENTION, and add a dispute-progress view with the 1 d countdown and evidence bundle
- Complete the money-in path for novices: clear TESTNET badging on faucets, address-book naming for recipients, and one shared 'prepare incoming capacity' component instead of three divergent copies
- Fill the empty screens: Ownership gets add-signer/threshold actions and shares state, Lending gets annualized rate + pool risk + utilization, Receive gets encoded-amount confirmation under the QR

### openrouter/x-ai/grok-latest · desktop-dark · 748

Desktop-dark is a coherent, high-trust operator console with a real one-scale bar language and excellent Pay/Sovereignty/Desk craft, but it still ships as a protocol workbench: empty faucets, credit sliders, and hex IDs sit in the first-money path. It would satisfy hubs and DeFi natives before it would onboard Maya, Lena, or a freelancer who needs a $20 invoice.

- Make spendable/enforceable vs promised the single hero everywhere; one Cover $90 CTA from Home, Account, Manage, Sovereignty.
- First-run money-in: hide faucets/credit exams; Amount-on-receive invoice; contacts not raw 0x on Pay.
- Cross-swap as a two-step wizard with one USD fee; disable Swap until inbound capacity exists.
- Receipt/Activity: one event, shareable PDF+proof pack, no contradictory background balances.
- Keep Desk/Jump/Lending/Ownership behind Comfort; default Home is Pay/Receive only.

### openrouter/anthropic/claude-sonnet-5 · mobile-dark · 795

The xln wallet has a coherent, well-thought-out visual language (green=yours, violet=promised) and genuinely strong trust/dispute/receipt screens that would satisfy security-conscious and treasury users. It falls short for first-timers and casual mobile users who face jargon (frames, lanes, capacity, atomic settlement) without enough inline explanation at the point of confusion.

- Add contextual one-line tooltips wherever jargon (frame, capacity, lane, atomic, promised) first appears instead of a single upfront explainer card
- Elevate critical trust/status lines (atomic settlement, nothing at risk, uncovered debt 'Cover it') with color/weight instead of same-style body text
- Reconcile duplicate/inconsistent counts and numbers across screens (movement counts, frame numbers) to preserve auditor trust
- Simplify disabled/zero-state cards (zero-balance Move cards, disabled Lend CTA) with dimming and inline helper text
- Surface Sovereignty/dispute-readiness closer to Home for security-focused users instead of 4 taps deep in Manage

### openrouter/x-ai/grok-latest · mobile-dark · 782

A dense, coherent dark system with a real trust story (green vs violet, receipts, dispute, sovereignty) that already feels like a specialist wallet, not a concept deck. It is not yet a shipping consumer product: jargon, clipped chrome, credit grants inside Swap, and a $155 vs $40 hero split will stall first-timers and treasurers.
