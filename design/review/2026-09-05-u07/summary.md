## Priority fixes by reviewer

### openrouter/google/gemini-flash-latest · desktop-dark · 672

The desktop dark interface maintains a cohesive design language with consistent technical styling and robust cryptographic status cues. However, it leans heavily on domain-specific abstractions and dense text blocks that hinder rapid comprehension for non-technical users.

- Add plain-language financial terms alongside protocol jargon across balances and transfers.
- Integrate clear shareable receipts and export flows directly into payment confirmations.
- Separate developer/faucet testing tools from production account and asset management.
- Improve visual hierarchy in dense views through adjustable column spacing and interactive filters.
- Provide step-by-step guided flows for dispute resolution and multi-signature operations.

### openrouter/google/gemini-flash-latest · mobile-dark · 851

The mobile dark UI achieves exceptional cryptographic rigor and sovereign clarity, visually communicating absolute scale and collateral boundaries better than any existing L2/state-channel wallet. However, it leans heavily on dense technical text and off-chain jargon that can overwhelm retail users while missing cross-chain routing and export actions needed by power treasurers.

- Simplify retail onboarding: replace technical explainers on Home with intuitive 'Secured / At Risk' balance pills and direct Deposit CTA.
- Add explicit transaction proof & bundle sharing actions (one-tap exportable JSON receipt + on-chain verification URL) on receipt screens.
- Expand the Receive flow with dynamic capacity management alerts so users can request collateral from hubs with one tap.
- Surface cross-chain bridge and network destination routes explicitly in the Swap & Pay flows (currently shows single Testnet/H1 paths).
- Refine touch targets, safe-area bottom insets, and horizontal scroll indicators across all mobile sheets and filter pills.

### openrouter/deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp · desktop-dark · 817

A genuinely premium dark product with a novel, honest risk model (one absolute bar scale, enforceable vs promised) — the core pay/receive/activity path is excellent. Trust and first-timer onboarding are undercut by dense jargon (credit room, lines out, bilateral-signed) and by two screens that read as dev or placeholder (Assets faucets, Lending's 100% per term), so it is strong for an expert but not yet a 3-minute story for a skeptic.

- Define or replace the core jargon in the payment path ('up to 175.00 instantly', 'credit room', 'lines out', 'bilateral-signed') with one plain sentence each — helps Maya, Sofia, Fatima, Dmitri
- Fix the Lending screen's '100bps = 100% per term' and the disabled-looking Offer button: show an annualized APR and an enabled-state with an inline reason — the single biggest trust-killer
- Compress the Assets screen's testnet Faucets block and replace the 100%/0% collateral slider with a plain 'if H1 defaults you lose $X' line — helps Anya, Ravi
- Rework the Desk lane table (and define 'lines out') and merge the duplicate stat cards — reduces density for Marcus and any non-operator
- Add an explicit invoice/Request flow and copy affordances on proof hashes — Sofia needs an invoice, not just a payment link; Ravi needs a clean, copyable proof

### zai-coding-cn/glm-5.3-flash · desktop-dark · 786

A visually coherent, trust-forward wallet with a genuinely differentiated green-vs-violet enforceability story, but it talks to experts in jargon on exactly the screens newcomers need, and several right-column cards visibly overflow or float over dead space. Consistency is high; information scent for non-crypto users and completion of secondary screens (Ownership, Lending) is where it falls short of shipping quality.

- Fix all right-edge overflow/clipping (05 book, 15 per-counterparty, 16 table/book, 09 scale demo) — broken layout reads as broken product
- Rewrite jargon on Receive, Assets, Lending and Ownership into plain language with tooltips (capacity, allowance, lazy entity, basis points)
- Replace duplicated text panels (How to read this, WHERE YOUR MONEY STANDS, Move's from/to cards) with compact visual components to recover space and reduce cognitive load
- Make trust claims interactive: proof hash copy, frame viewer links, evidence bundle preview
- Fill or restructure the pervasive empty right/lower space (04, 10, 11, 16) with actionable content: invoices, disputes, open orders

### openrouter/qwen/qwen3.8-flash · desktop-dark · 797

This is a coherent, premium, genuinely novel wallet UI whose single-absolute-scale bars and 'enforceable-now' framing are a real differentiator that a skeptical investor and a security maximalist can both read off the screens. It is held back by a few data contradictions (collateral vs secured/covered), horizontal clipping on the densest views (Desk, Sovereignty), wasted vertical space on desktop, and jargon density that blocks first-timers and cross-border senders on the Move/Assets paths.

- Resolve the collateral/secured/covered contradiction: one lane-derived number shown identically on Home, Account and Sovereignty (06 vs 01 vs 15).
- Fix horizontal overflow: make the Desk table's 'LINES OUT·IN' column and the Sovereignty per-counterparty text fully visible (scroll, wrap, or drawer).
- Give Move an explicit network/chain selector and a visible cross-chain fee so Fatima's task is completable from that screen.
- De-jargon the novice path: human-readable fees ('$0.00'), one plain 'what you can spend' line on Home/Pay, and split the overloaded Assets/Faucet panel into three intents.
