# Crypto liquidity and participation incentives in November 2026

Research cutoff: 13 November 2026, Asia/Bangkok. This report examines five selected market directions through their capital suppliers, distribution, incentive payers and loss allocation. It accompanies the [XLN settlement cases and unmet-demand research](xln-market-research-2026-11-12.md).

## Main findings

Robinhood is a substantial operating financial business; the examined evidence does not establish a pump-and-dump involving the company or its new chain. Its product marketing still deserves scrutiny, particularly the rights conveyed by Stock Tokens and the components and risks of advertised lending yield. Company earnings do not establish the separate profitability of its chain.

The common mechanism across the stronger cases is distribution attached to a useful financial service. Robinhood can move an existing customer base and acquire networks. Hyperliquid lets applications and market deployers monetize trading flow. Relay and LI.FI compensate integrating applications. Prediction markets pay for executable depth. Stablecoin networks distribute reserve income and finance actual payment timing gaps.

Cash promotion, customer-paid fees, borrower interest, trading P&L and token allocations are different funding sources. A product can combine genuine demand with speculative participation and substantial acquisition spending. None of those observations alone establishes manipulation or durable profitability.

| Direction | Who brings capital | Why participants join | What must survive reduced incentives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Robinhood and tokenized finance | Existing brokerage customers, acquired exchange networks, depositors and professional makers | Convenience, product access, transfer matches, distribution rewards and yield | Product-level contribution margins and retained customer assets |
| Hyperliquid | Traders, HLP depositors, makers and builders | Execution, fees/spreads, rebates, builder income and historical token upside | Paying order flow and LP returns after tail losses |
| Relay and open intents | Solvers, liquidity providers and integrating applications | Inventory returns, application fees, revenue sharing and better conversion | Profitable fills after funding, rebalancing and sponsorship costs |
| Prediction markets | Traders and market makers | Event exposure, spreads, depth rewards and fee rebates | Useful depth and fee-paying activity without circular incentive trades |
| Stablecoin credit and payments | Reserve capital, payment lenders and institutional/retail LPs | Redemption, payment access, interest, distribution income and token rewards | Borrower repayment and net yield independent of reward-token prices |

## Method and evidence limits

Primary filings, product terms, current documentation, operator announcements and original research take priority over token price, social engagement and promotional rankings. Searches included `site:investors.robinhood.com 2026 third quarter results November 2026`, `site:docs.polymarket.com liquidity rewards maker rebates 2026 fees`, and `site:huma.finance 2026 payment financing fees liquidity providers losses`. Protocol indexes were used to locate current pages rather than relying on guessed paths.

Living documentation can contain retired products. This report separates a source's publication date from the current text and labels announcements or code capabilities that are not evidence of widespread production use. Robinhood, Relay and Huma metrics below are issuer/operator statements unless explicitly described otherwise. No reconciled cross-platform dataset or subsidy-removal experiment was available, so the report makes no current market-share or investment-valuation ranking.

Direct retrieval of Hyperliquid's original JELLY announcement returned 403. The relevant governance risk is documented through a public securities filing. One Kalshi volume-incentive help URL returned 404; the prediction-market comparison below uses accessible Kalshi depth-program terms and current Polymarket rebate documentation instead of treating that unavailable page as current policy.

## Robinhood has a real business and purchases distribution

### Operating performance and the crypto narrative

Robinhood reported Q3 2026 revenue of $1.42 billion, net income of $476 million, $16 billion in net deposits and 5.2 million Gold subscribers. Crypto revenue fell 55% year over year to $120 million, while event-contract revenue rose more than sixfold to $173 million. The quarter ended 30 September; results were published on 27 October. These figures support a diversified operating business, not a claim that chain activity alone caused company growth. [Q3 results](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-third-quarter-2026-results).

The fair distinction is between a functioning business, a potentially expensive growth campaign, a speculative asset valuation and an allegation of coordinated manipulation. The examined evidence supports the first two. It does not establish that Robinhood's equity, Chain or Stock Tokens form a pump-and-dump. Nor does it establish that any quoted asset price is justified.

### How customers and liquidity arrive

Robinhood starts with an existing customer base. Its September-October 2026 ACATS campaign offered personalized 1%, 2% or 3% cash matches, with a 3% offer for qualifying Webull transfers. The promotion ended on 16 October. Five-year proportional chargeback conditions encourage asset retention; this is not the same as making all principal inaccessible for five years. The economic test is retained contribution after the cash match, servicing and funding costs. [Promotion terms](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/2026-Hood-Month-ACAT-Match/).

Robinhood also acquired distribution. The completed Bitstamp purchase brought existing customers, institutional relationships and exchange infrastructure. Buying a functioning order-flow network is materially different from attracting every counterparty to an empty venue. [Acquisition completed 2 June 2025](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-completes-acquisition-of-bitstamp/).

Current documentation describes Robinhood Chain as an Arbitrum-based permissionless network with ETH gas. The July launch connected tokenized assets with trading and lending infrastructure. Bringing balances from an existing brokerage and recruiting exchanges, makers and lending partners can accelerate the initial market; it does not disclose the private price paid to recruit each partner. [Chain documentation](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/), [July 2026 launch](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-accelerates-global-expansion-robinhood-chain-mainnet-stock-tokens-agentic-trading/).

### Who pays for USDG adoption and Earn yield

Global Dollar Network rewards participating distributors for minting, holding and accepting USDG. Reserve income gives wallets and exchanges an economic reason to promote this particular stablecoin. Any end-user benefit from reserve rewards depends on what the distributor passes through. Reserve rewards and adoption incentives should be separated when calculating the network's acquisition cost. [GDN economics](https://globaldollar.com/network), [July 2026 partner update](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/150-partners).

Robinhood Earn supplies USDG through a Morpho vault. Its documentation says variable APY combines borrower payments and additional Morpho rewards; it does not provide a precise split behind an estimated 7% display. The policy described as insurance covers Robinhood, not a direct individual-user claim, and excludes borrower-default and liquidity/governance losses. A no-lockup interface is not a guarantee of immediate stressed redemption. [Earn mechanics and risks](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/crypto-earn/).

The commercial capital path is therefore: customer assets and incentives bring balances; stablecoin distribution economics help retain them; lenders supply capital; borrowers pay for access; additional reward programmes can supplement returns. Fees, credit losses and promotional costs determine whether each participant actually benefits.

### Token rights and fair criticism

The Q3 10-Q distinguishes July 2026 Stock Tokens, which are tokenized debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited with underlying collateral requirements, from Classic Stock Tokens, which are bilateral contracts for which underlying holdings are hedges rather than required backing. Neither grants underlying-company shareholder rights. Price exposure through an issuer obligation should not be described as ordinary direct share ownership. [Q3 10-Q filed 28 October 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000167/hood-20260930.htm).

In January 2025 the SEC imposed $45 million in combined penalties on Robinhood broker-dealers for multiple compliance and operational failures. That is material adverse evidence about controls. It is not a finding that Robinhood Chain or its tokens constitute a pump-and-dump. [SEC release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-5).

The missing success tests are product-level contribution margins, deposit cohorts after promotion expiration, the split between borrower-funded and reward-funded APY, negotiated maker support, and customer execution quality. Gross transfers, DEX volume and company-wide profits cannot answer those questions separately.

## Hyperliquid recruits both risk capital and distributors

### Historical bootstrap and current income

The official points history describes a programme from November 2023 to May 2024 and an L1 phase from May to November 2024, including affiliate allocations. This was explicit early-user and referral acquisition machinery. The current page does not establish a new official points season. [Points history](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/points).

HLP provides community-funded market-making and liquidation capital, earns part of trading fees, and currently also supplies USDC to Earn. Depositors share strategy profits and losses; the latest deposit resets a four-day lock. Its return is compensation for risk and useful capital, not a guaranteed savings coupon. [Current HLP](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/protocol-vaults).

Referrers receive a documented share of referred-user fees and users receive a discount. Builder codes let applications charge a user-approved fee on routed fills. Both mechanisms give third parties a reason to bring paying flow rather than only advertise the protocol's technology. [Referrals](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/referrals), [builder codes](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/builder-codes).

Makers receive fee rebates, while HIP-3 growth mode reduces specified fees and related rebates by 90%. Rebates purchase liquidity; discounts sacrifice fee income; historical points allocated potential token value. These costs must remain distinct. The assistance fund's HYPE purchases and burns are also separate from cash distributed to liquidity providers. [Fee mechanics](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees).

### The builder incentive loop

HIP-3 lets deployers create markets and manage their oracles while reusing exchange infrastructure. The documented requirement is 500,000 staked HYPE, maintained for at least 183 days. Deployers can earn configured fees, giving specialist teams an incentive to bring markets, customers and makers. Slashed stake is burned rather than automatically paying affected users. [HIP-3](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals).

The hypothesized growth loop is shared initial liquidity, useful execution, paying order flow, compensation for makers and distributors, and then deeper liquidity. Its components are documented; attributing a particular percentage of growth to any component would require cohort evidence.

### Who loses in stress

HLP bears inventory and liquidation outcomes. ADL can close profitable opposite positions when needed to resolve underwater exposure; the documented invariant protects users without open positions from that socialization. A trader using a position as a hedge can therefore suffer a forced-close cost even when the platform avoids outstanding bad debt. [ADL](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/auto-deleveraging).

The March 2025 JELLY intervention illustrates governance discretion over stressed market settlement. A public securities filing discusses the delisting and selected settlement price as a neutrality risk. Conflicting reported loss and unrealized-exposure numbers are not combined here. [SEC-filed risk discussion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088139/000121390026052317/ea0288809-s1a3_bitwise.htm).

Current vault documentation distinguishes HyperEVM vaults with spot/HIP-3 access from legacy HyperCore vault limits. Native unified/portfolio margin also closes part of the old balance-fragmentation problem. Institutional cross-venue financing is already offered through Ripple Prime. [Current vaults](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults), [portfolio margin](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/portfolio-margin), [Ripple Prime integration](https://ripple.com/ripple-press/ripple-announces-support-for-hyperliquid-expanding-institutional-access-to-onchain-liquidity/).

Durability requires fee income after rebates and referrals, retained activity after token expectations diminish, and HLP returns including stressed drawdowns. Current volume-quality and incentive-removal measurements were not established by this research.

## Relay and open intents attach economics to distribution

### Relay's application and solver capital loop

Relay's current announcement names existing wallet and application integrations and describes Relay Chain as live with gradual rollout. These are distribution and capital-rotation mechanisms. Its cumulative volume and transaction claims are operator-reported and are not a count of retained independent customers. [Relay announcement](https://www.relay.link/blog/relay-raises-series-b).

A solver advances destination assets and receives a Hub balance after attestation, then chooses withdrawal timing. Quoting and order distribution sit outside Relay Settlement's core flow. Cross-chain speed consequently involves capital advanced by an intermediary; it does not eliminate every connected chain's finality and inventory constraints. [Protocol mechanics](https://docs.relay.link/references/protocol/how-it-works.md).

Relay's fees separate execution, swapping/rebalancing, platform and integrator app charges. Standard platform categories currently list 1 bp for stablecoin swaps, 6 bp for major swaps and 15 bp for minor swaps. Qualified integrators completing KYB and exceeding $10 million in trailing-30-day volume can share platform fees. An application can also add its own fee. [Fee schedule](https://docs.relay.link/references/api/api_core_concepts/fees.md).

Applications may use earned app fees or separately deposited funds to sponsor user charges. The platform-fee revenue share has separate terms prohibiting its use to discount the Relay product. The distribution bargain remains explicit: the wallet brings users, earns fees and may fund acquisition. Sponsorship is an integrator expense, not free yield. [App balance](https://relay.link/app-balance), [integration guide](https://relay.link/blog/how-to-integrate-relay-api), [revenue-share conditions](https://docs.relay.link/references/api/api_core_concepts/fees.md).

The security model still contains an Oracle, an MPC Allocator and a Security Council capable of halting new withdrawal-proof generation. Describing this as an independently enforceable bilateral exit would erase material trust assumptions. Protocol role descriptions also do not measure the number of independent active solvers. [Security model](https://docs.relay.link/references/protocol/security.md).

Relay Vaults are winding down: new deposits are disabled and the withdrawal UI is available until 31 December 2026. Historical vault descriptions are not a current offer. [Vault notice](https://docs.relay.link/references/protocol/vaults/overview).

### Open standards still need order flow and balance sheets

LI.FI announced LI.FI Intents on OIF reference contracts in May 2026. Open tooling lowers integration cost, while an established application network can supply initial orders. LI.FI's general monetization documentation describes a service fee, optional integrator fees and volume discounts; it does not establish one universal price for every enterprise or intent route. [Launch](https://li.fi/knowledge-hub/a-more-intentional-ethereum-li.fi-intents-and-the-open-intents-framework), [fees](https://docs.li.fi/faqs/fees-monetization).

Solver participation still requires inventory, reliable fills and proofs before expiry. Order-server discovery and allocator signatures for Compact flows mean open contracts do not automatically provide access to all commercial order flow. [Solver requirements](https://docs.li.fi/lifi-intents/for-solvers/intro).

OIF already describes order purchasing / underwriting for faster capital rotation. Liquorice describes off-chain matching and inventory lending, with proceeds and maker collateral supporting the debt and fee income compensating LPs. These are direct competitors to a general claim that solvers have execution tools but no financing infrastructure. [OIF contracts](https://github.com/openintentsframework/oif-contracts), [Liquorice flow](https://liquorice.gitbook.io/liquorice-docs/intro/general-flow).

### Everclear separates customer-paid rebalancing from token support

Everclear's mechanics net opposite flows and discount unmatched invoices until supplying opposite liquidity becomes attractive. In its illustrative 100-to-99 example, the impatient party receives 99 and the supplying participant receives the difference. The customer needing settlement funds that incentive; gas and other costs remain additional. [Mechanics](https://docs.everclear.org/developers/protocol-mechanics).

The September 2024 beta launch named professional participants including Tokka and Anera. Tokenomics separately describes staked CLEAR voting power directing emissions toward routes, attracting solvers before sufficient organic volume exists. The document remains subject to review, so it is not evidence of a verified current emission budget or profitability. [Launch history](https://blog.everclear.org/everclears-mainnet-is-live-24dedd572d56), [tokenomics](https://dao-docs.everclear.org/clear/clear-tokenomics).

Customer-paid discounts and treasury-token distribution must be accounted for separately. Distributing a capped supply is not necessarily new minting, but still spends treasury value and changes circulating supply. The test is retained rebalancing participation and customer savings after route incentives decline.

## Prediction markets explicitly buy liquidity

### Polymarket pays for depth and executed activity differently

Liquidity rewards score qualifying size, tight quotes and two-sided books. Payments are market-specific, and listing a market does not by itself establish an active budget. Native and sponsored funding exist. The special August crypto TWAP campaign has ended and is not current acquisition spending. Makers still bear inventory and adverse-selection losses. [Liquidity rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/liquidity-rewards).

Current Polymarket documentation includes category-dependent taker fees and maker rebates funded from those fees; geopolitical/world-event markets remain fee-free. Fees depend on price and contract quantity, so the headline parameter is not a flat percentage of dollar notional. [Fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees).

The separate taker programme offers prospective rebates and tier bonuses based on eligible rolling volume. It excludes inauthentic activity under its rules. The economic distinction matters: one programme purchases resting depth, another redistributes fees from executed trades, and another incentivizes fee-paying taker volume. None should automatically be called subsidy-independent adoption. [Taker rebates](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/taker-rebates).

Original research by Allen Sirolly, Hongyao Ma, Yash Kanoria and Rajiv Sethi identifies trading networks consistent with wash activity. Its estimates concern historical data through 2025, not a November 2026 measurement or adjudicated misconduct. The authors note that legitimate automated market making can resemble the patterns being detected. [Columbia research](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/network-based-detection-wash-trading).

### Kalshi makes the purchase of order-book quality explicit

Kalshi's liquidity incentive programme rewards resting orders even when unfilled, with size, price distance and two-sided depth affecting scores. Its separate designated-LP programme lets applicants quote the compensation they require to meet liquidity obligations. This is an explicit procurement process for a useful market service. Programme budgets and participation conditions can change. [Liquidity incentive programme](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program), [designated providers](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program).

Current published reward schedules demonstrate concrete budget allocation rather than a vague claim that liquidity appears naturally. They do not establish net platform profits or the source of every dollar paid. [Published schedules](https://kalshi.com/incentives).

The success test is executable depth, spreads and paying-user retention after rewards, with participant returns measured after losses. Volume alone is especially weak evidence when the incentive design pays for volume.

## Stablecoin payments compete through distribution and real credit

### Reserve income pays for acceptance and settlement availability

Circle's October 2026 explanation describes distribution partnerships, qualifying redemption terms and pre-positioned bank cash that supports near-real-time redemptions. That cash earns less than Treasuries; liquidity availability has a real funding cost. Moving prefunding away from a customer does not remove the need for liquid capital somewhere in the network. [Circle economics, 16 October 2026](https://www.circle.com/blog/the-economics-of-usdc-and-why-our-partners-win).

The current CPN page reports more than 200 enrolled institutions as of 4 November 2026 and offers managed, fiat and stablecoin payment products. Enrollment and announced country reach are not active paying-counterparty counts or corridor-level profitability. The distribution proposition is one integration plus issuer, banking and payout relationships. [Current CPN](https://www.circle.com/cpn).

For USDG, the previously described reserve-income sharing gives distribution partners a direct incentive to promote balances. For USDC/CPN, service access and qualifying redemption economics can similarly encourage customer balances and integration. The individual contracts determine the actual acquisition cost and how much benefit reaches the customer.

### Huma combines payment-financing demand with LP rewards

Huma's August 2026 update describes short-duration payment financing through Arf and a separate trade-finance business. It reports more than $17 billion cumulative volume and no credit defaults to date; these remain company statements, not this report's audited loss history. Repeated short loans recycle the same capital, so cumulative financed volume must not be interpreted as unique capital supplied. [Business update, 25 August 2026](https://blog.huma.finance/huma-built-payfi.-now-institutions-build-on-huma).

The underlying economic payer is a business that values access to liquidity. LPs receive cash yield from financing activity and can receive separate HUMA rewards. The Feathers documentation says reward APY uses the seven-day average HUMA price, making that component price-dependent rather than a fixed USDC coupon. [Reward mechanics](https://docs.huma.finance/products/huma-2.0/feathers.md).

There is important documentation drift. A general product page still describes Classic and Maxi choices, but the dated April 2026 announcement disables new Maxi deposits and switches and ends auto-renewal. Existing reward commitments persist according to their terms. The same announcement moves the first team/investor unlock to 26 November 2026 and describes future governance plans. Those are dated commitments, not proof of future execution. [Old product overview](https://docs.huma.finance/products/huma-2.0/overview.md), [April changes](https://blog.huma.finance/huma-build-to-last).

Institutional first-loss-cover documentation describes extra collateral and reserve capital absorbing specified losses before lenders, subject to caps and pool configuration. Insurance is described there as a future consideration, not universal current coverage. The existence of the design does not establish the protection of a particular retail position. [Institutional loss allocation](https://docs.huma.finance/products/huma-institutional/first-loss-covers.md).

The durability test is cash interest and fees received after realized losses, funding and servicing costs, without relying on reward-token appreciation. Looping a yield token through other lending venues can increase leverage and reported capital measures without adding the same amount of independent underlying payment demand.

## What the problem discussions have in common

The immediate users' experience can become simple while professional intermediaries still fund inventory, retain liquidity and accept timing/default risk. Interfaces hide those costs; they do not eliminate them. Relay's solver advances, Circle's bank cash, HLP's backstop capital and Huma's funded credit are different instances of this requirement.

Access remains selective at commercially important boundaries. Application revenue sharing can require minimum flow and KYB, prime brokerage depends on counterparty acceptance, and solver bonding can immobilize operating capital. The earlier research preserves first-person solver examples, while distinguishing current-year complaints from historical requests.

Open-source contracts commoditize part of implementation. Distribution, trusted execution, credit judgment and balance sheets remain scarce. A protocol can be technically permissionless while its valuable order-flow channels, funding or exits involve specific operators and conditions.

The financial question behind a convincing success is therefore: who earns enough to keep supplying the needed role after acquisition spending and token expectations decline? A high asset balance can reflect customer usefulness, a cash match, speculative yield, leverage, acquired assets or price appreciation. These explanations require different evidence.

## Implications for XLN

The most transferable lesson is to give every necessary participant an identifiable economic reason to join. An application needs revenue or improved conversion; a solver needs a positive spread after inventory costs; a creditor needs acceptable compensation and enforceable agreed rights; a merchant needs useful completed commerce. Token incentives cannot substitute for all four.

The narrower opportunity is repeated bilateral finance where counterparties already trade and an agreed credit relationship becomes cheaper or easier to service. Compare against Wildcat/private loans, existing solver-credit products and the relevant incumbent settlement network. XLN does not create a lender, replace venue margin rules or guarantee unsecured repayment. Its release scope is unchanged by this research.

For MML, distinguish useful obligations between unaffiliated entities from technical fills, route hops, rebalances, speculative turnover and farming loops. A measured commercial edge should be counted once. None of the selected platforms' headline volume claims establishes GDP attribution.

## Confidence and remaining evidence

Confidence is high in the documented incentive mechanisms and the distinctions between current and retired products. Confidence is lower in causal growth attribution, subsidy-free retention and product-level risk-adjusted profits. Important numbers are corporate disclosures or operator statements with their original dates, not independently reproduced platform measurements.

The strongest next evidence would be retained cohorts after programmes end, actual capital and counterparty concentration, all fees after rebates and sponsorship, and returns after credit/market losses. For Robinhood specifically, Chain contribution margins and Earn's interest/reward split remain open. For Hyperliquid, HLP tail-risk returns and net incentive costs matter. For intents, measure solver net margins and independent participation. For prediction markets, measure incentive-adjusted execution quality. For PayFi, reconcile borrower repayments, reward distributions and defaults by cohort.

The overall conclusion is that real products, speculation and paid acquisition coexist. The reviewed evidence supports substantial businesses and useful financial mechanisms. It does not justify treating every token narrative as fraud, or every growing volume/TVL chart as durable economic success.
